## RQ1 — source-progress safety across Kafka, files, and syslog spools

**Answer: safety yes for v0.7.0, within the tested cases; no for v0.6.0.
One liveness failure on Kafka.**

- **Model (E-RQ1-M).** The commit rule (I1) held everywhere. Source-progress
  safety (I2) held for v0.7.0 in all 8 configurations with one batch per
  unit per cycle, up to 793,353 states (4 ranges, 2 failures, 2 crashes,
  ledger-tail loss, storage mutation). v0.6.0 violated I2 after a single
  verification failure with no crash (32 states): the failed range is not
  handed back and the next range commits past it. v0.7.0 with two batches
  of one unit in a cycle also violates I2, so its safety rests on one
  pending buffer per Kafka partition and per file generation, which the
  code provides.
- **Fault injection (E-RQ1-F).** 49 trials, 285 random SIGKILLs, 42
  storage or broker outages, 80 power-loss emulations: **0 unsafe
  commits** at every crash snapshot and at the end. Every committed
  position was covered by verified, byte-exact objects. 48 of 49 archives
  were complete at the end; the exception is the Kafka stall below.

| Scenario | Trials | Kills | Unsafe | Complete | Duplicate objects / trial (median [95% CI]) | Replay amplification (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| file-mix (Linux+SSH+BGL, 4 MiB) | 10 | 59 | 0 | 10/10 | 0 [0, 0] | 0.0% |
| spool-mix (Linux+SSH, 1 MiB) | 10 | 51 | 0 | 10/10 | 0 [0, 0.5] | 0.0% |
| power-loss (file-mix + WAL deleted) | 10 | 80 | 0 | 10/10 | 108 [94, 119] | 108.9% |
| HDFS 1.58 GB (16 MiB) | 5 | 40 | 0 | 5/5 | 0 [0, 0] | 0.0% |
| Kafka SSH, 1 partition | 8 | 31 | 0 | 8/8 | 0 [0, 0] | 0.0% |
| Kafka HDFS 2 M records, 4 partitions | 6 | 24 | 0 | **5/6** | 0 [0, 0] | 0.0% |

- **Kafka stall (finding F5).** In trial `hdfs-p4-t02` the final engine
  process read partition 0 contiguously to offset 447,960, committed it,
  and then received nothing more from that partition for the 900 s
  drain, polling empty every 250 ms with no warning; partitions 1–3
  finished. The broker's committed offset stayed at 447,961, equal to the
  verified prefix, so this is a liveness failure, not data loss: the
  remaining 80,181 records stayed in Kafka, and restarting the engine on
  the same ledger and consumer group archived and committed them in 2 s.
  The Kafka adapter is identical in `a8bce8e` and the tag (0 of 14 stalls
  in the first round), so this is intermittent, not a regression. Root
  cause not identified; the trial's ledger export and engine logs are in
  `experiments/rq1-source-safety/results/20261110-v070-kafka/stall-hdfs-p4-t02/`.
- **Duplicates (H1.4).** At-least-once confirmed. Random kills rarely
  create duplicates (two spool-mix trials with 1 and 2), because the kill
  must land in the ms-scale window between upload and commit. Power loss
  is the expensive case: the ledger rolls back to its last checkpoint,
  and 50–129% of the source is archived again.
- **Restart latency.** Median time from restart to first new commit:
  0.05 s (files), 0.16 s (HDFS, 16 MiB batches), 1.04 s (syslog spool).
- **First round (`a8bce8e`):** 49 trials, 281 kills, 43 outages, 80 WAL
  deletions, 0 unsafe, 49/49 complete; power-loss median 118 duplicate
  objects, 119% amplification.

## RQ2 — detection of corrupted or replaced evidence during recovery

**Answer: yes with strong verification (64/64 mutations refused); size-only
verification commits same-size corruption.** Identical outcome in every
cell on the tag and on `a8bce8e`.

| Mutation (while engine down, batch VERIFIED) | Strong | Strong+MAC | Size-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| M0 none (control) | committed 4/4 | committed 4/4 | committed 4/4 |
| M1 flip one object byte | 4/4 | 4/4 | **0/4 (corruption committed)** |
| M2 truncate object | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| M3 delete object | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| M4 replace object with another batch's | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| M5 edit manifest, stale self-hash | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| M6 edit manifest, recomputed self-hash | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| M7 forge object + manifest, recomputed | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |
| M8 delete manifest | 4/4 | 4/4 | 4/4 |

Every detected mutation was re-read from the source and re-archived; the
final archive was complete. Forged manifests (M6, M7) were caught
**without** the MAC because the ledger stores the object and manifest
digests (ledger binding). The MAC's separate value could not be isolated
in this threat model (attacker writes only to object storage).

**V-norecheck causal ablation (2026-11-11).** A separate build skips
`storage_still_verified` without reading the stored evidence. All 32 mutations (plus 4 unmutated controls) were accepted, including 24 unsafe commits across M1–M4, M7, and M8.
This directly demonstrates that restart revalidation is the barrier against
committing stale or corrupted evidence. See
`experiments/rq2-corruption-recovery/results/20261111-v070-norecheck/ANALYSIS.md`.

## RQ3 — cost of verification-bound commits

**Answer: the verification work itself is small when objects are
compressed (0.1–1.4 ms per 16 MiB batch against 13.5–24.6 ms of
compression) and an order of magnitude larger without compression
(12.8–15.1 ms), because read-back verification hashes the stored bytes.
End-to-end throughput differences for compressed objects are within the
run-to-run spread of this host; the uncompressed penalty (12–20%) is not.**

Continuous-engine steady-state throughput, LocalFS backend, 16 MiB
batches, 10 reps (median MiB/s, change relative to size-only; 95% CI in
`analysis/tables/rq3_cost.tex`):

| Dataset | size-only | SHA-256 | BLAKE3 | SHA-256+MAC | SHA-256, no compression |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SSH | 330.7 | 323.5 (−2.2%) | 322.3 (−2.5%) | 327.2 (−1.1%) | 263.1 (−20.4%) |
| BGL | 368.8 | 339.1 (−8.0%) | 332.1 (−9.9%) | 344.5 (−6.6%) | 317.5 (−13.9%) |
| HDFS | 352.1 | 302.5 (−14.1%) | 319.6 (−9.2%) | 332.9 (−5.5%) | 308.9 (−12.3%) |

The SHA-256 confidence intervals overlap size-only for SSH and HDFS. The
first round on `a8bce8e`, whose throughput path is the same code, gave
−3.4%, −4.2%, and +2.6% for SHA-256 and −14.0%, −7.2%, −13.1% without
compression. Read the compressed-object columns as "no effect larger than
the spread between runs", not as a point estimate; the uncompressed
penalty is consistent in both rounds.

- **Where the time goes** (`process-once`, mean ms per 16 MiB batch, tag):
  compression 13.5–24.6 ms; checksum + verify 0.1–1.4 ms with zstd,
  12.8–15.1 ms without compression; ledger writes 0.2–1.1 ms; adapter
  commit < 0.5 ms. Same pattern in the first round (24–37 ms, 1.6–3.9 ms,
  18–22 ms).
- **CPU:** 2.74–3.21 s per GiB for compressed variants, 3.22–3.74 s/GiB
  uncompressed. Peak RSS ~79 MiB (SSH), ~192 MiB (BGL), ~312 MiB (HDFS).
- **MAC:** under 1 ms per batch; throughput +1.1%, +1.6%, +10.1% vs.
  SHA-256 (SSH, BGL, HDFS), inside the spread.
- **Batch size** (median MiB/s): BGL 163 (1 MiB), 298 (4), 340 (16), 344
  (64); HDFS 158, 293, 334, 348. Manifest bytes as a share of stored
  bytes (BGL): 2.41%, 0.59%, 0.149%, 0.040%. The first round showed the
  same shape (BGL 210 → 377).
- Figures: `analysis/figures/rq3-throughput.pdf`, `rq3-stages.pdf`,
  `rq3-batchsize.pdf`.

### S3 over a local Docker network (clean assembly, 2026-11-11)

**Answer: service-computed SHA-256 has no resolved throughput penalty against
size-only on any dataset. After the 60 overlap-exposed runs were replaced,
BLAKE3 read-back has no resolved penalty on any dataset either. Without
compression the penalty remains large and resolved on all three datasets.**
This is S3 over a local Docker network, not public-cloud S3 or a distributed
deployment.

The clean dataset contains 90 original unexposed runs and 60 quiet-host
replacement runs (SSH rep 0, BGL reps 5–9, and HDFS reps 4–9). The original
150 records and their analysis remain preserved but are not used for
manuscript claims because three chunks overlapped other experiments. Every
replacement run has a timestamp and a quiet-environment check. Every clean
run drained, and the first repetition of every cell passed the independent
S3 digest-and-byte-exact coverage oracle (15/15). Driver:
`experiments/rq3-cost-ablation/code/exp_minio_cost.py`; clean records and
analysis: `experiments/rq3-cost-ablation/results/20261111-v070-minio-cost-clean/`.

| Dataset | size-only | Service SHA-256 | BLAKE3 read-back | SHA-256+MAC | SHA-256, no compression |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SSH | 202.3 | 194.6 | 189.2 | 189.6 | 75.1 |
| BGL | 280.1 | 287.7 | 255.1 | 282.4 | 95.8 |
| HDFS | 274.0 | 289.4 | 262.0 | 280.4 | 91.2 |

Median steady-state MiB/s, 10 runs per cell. Percentile-bootstrap 95% CIs are
in the clean directory's `summary.json` and `ANALYSIS.md`.

- **Service SHA-256:** drops of 3.81% (SSH), −2.71% (BGL), and −5.61%
  (HDFS) against size-only; every CI includes zero.
- **BLAKE3 read-back:** drops of 6.46%, 8.92%, and 4.38%, respectively;
  every CI includes zero. The earlier overlap-affected 10.39% and 12.59%
  claims are excluded and must not be used.
- **Without compression:** drops of 62.86% (95% CI 58.79–69.25%), 65.81%
  (62.02–69.37%), and 66.72% (61.16–70.28%), all resolved.
- **MAC:** no resolved difference from service SHA-256 without MAC on any
  dataset; all three CIs include zero.
- **Read-back volume:** BLAKE3 reads the complete stored object bodies, a
  median of 4.914, 67.203, and 145.919 MiB per run (SSH, BGL, HDFS). Service
  SHA-256 does not download the object body for verification.
- Native-S3 detailed stage fields can be zero in v0.7.0; those zeros are not
  reported as stage costs. HTTP request counts were not observed and are not
  inferred.

## RQ4 — comparison with an existing pipeline (one baseline, D2)

Confluent S3 Sink 12.1.15 on Confluent Platform 7.9.0, exactly-once configuration (deterministic
partitioner, rotation every 40,000 records), against VTOP v0.7.0 (`s3_native`, BLAKE3, strong
verification); same Kafka 3.9.1 topic, MinIO (RELEASE.2025-09-07, built from source), S3 fault
proxy and oracle. Configuration: `experiments/rq4-baselines/configs/README.md`.

| Fault | System | Trials (fault applied) | Unsafe | Committed records lost | Complete | Dup. obj. (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ack-corrupted | VTOP | 10 (of 12 run) | 0 | 0 | 9/10 | 0 |
| ack-corrupted | Confluent | 10 | 10 | 400,000 | 0/10 | 0 |
| ack-lost | VTOP | 10 | 0 | 0 | 9/10 | 0 |
| ack-lost | Confluent | 10 | 10 | 400,000 | 0/10 | 0 |
| SIGKILL ×3 | VTOP | 5 (15 kills) | 0 | 0 | 5/5 | 2 |
| SIGKILL ×3 | Confluent | 5 (13 kills) | 0 | 0 | 5/5 | 0 |

- Confluent committed its offset past the acknowledged-but-damaged object in every storage-fault
  trial (40,000 committed records without an intact copy each time): the tested connector path did
  not detect the mutation or deletion before committing.
- VTOP's verification failed the affected batch and re-read the range: no unsafe commit in 27 trials.
  This also covers E-RQ2-C (corruption between upload and verification) for the Kafka source and the
  native S3 backend.
- VTOP stalled on Kafka in 4 of 27 trials (finding 6 below, vtop-engine #623; mechanism and evidence in
  `stall-ssh-p1-corrupt-t00/`); 2 stalls came before the fault was injected and those trials are not
  counted for the fault. Safe: the offset stayed at the verified prefix.
- Elapsed times are not a performance result (shared host, buffering proxy; `ENVIRONMENT-NOTES.md`).
- Vector, Fluent Bit and Flink were not run.

## Findings about VTOP itself

1. **Manifest under-states its source range.** A batch built from several
   reads gets the last read's marker in its manifest, so the manifest's
   start is later than the object's first record: 3,955 of 4,056
   manifests in the tag's crash experiments (98%; files and spools 3,569
   of 3,633, Kafka 386 of 423). Batch identifiers carry the same
   under-stated start. Ledger-based recovery is unaffected; manifest-only
   audit is not. Still present at the tag: `crates/vtop-cli/src/engine.rs`
   ≈ L666 (`source_progress: read.progress_end.for_manifest()`).
2. **v0.6.0 had a single-failure safety gap**, reproduced in the model;
   v0.7.0 closes it (VTOP #552, #558, #563).
3. **Power loss is safe but costly** under `synchronous=NORMAL`.
4. **The ledger, not the self-hash, is the integrity anchor** for
   recovery.
5. **Size-only verification is a real integrity loss**, not a cosmetic
   downgrade.
6. **Intermittent Kafka partition stall** in a running engine (1 of 28
   Kafka trials across both rounds; 4 of 27 VTOP trials in E-RQ4-B), cleared
   by a restart; safe, but a liveness gap. A partition held at a concrete
   offset stops delivering after a pause/resume, and v0.7.0 never re-assigns
   it (vtop-engine #623; fixed after v0.7.0 by PR #644, merged as 6c01088; the exact trigger in
   our trials is still not reproduced).

  no file replacement; it does not cover same-inode replacement.
